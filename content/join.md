Sentometrics is a small field with a low barrier to entry: the methods are documented, the software
is on CRAN, and several indices are published here for anyone to use. What follows is what you need
to start.

## Start here

1. Read the [survey](https://sentometrics-research.com/publication/econometrics-meets-sentiment/) — it maps the whole chain from
   corpus to econometric model, and tells you which step your question actually belongs to.
2. Install [**sentometrics**](https://sentometricsresearch.github.io/sentometrics) and work through
   the [JSS vignette](https://sentometrics-research.com/publication/sentometrics-r-package/).
3. Pick an index from [Indices & data](https://sentometrics-research.com/#download) and reproduce a result from the paper behind it.
   This is the fastest way to find out whether the field suits you.
4. When you want to go wider, use the [reading list](https://sentometrics-research.com/reading/).

## Data you can build on

Every index we publish is documented, versioned, and free for academic use. They are deliberately
reusable as starting points:

- **[MCCC](https://sentometrics-research.com/download/mccc/)** — daily US media climate change concerns, 2003 to present, with
  topical and thematic subindices. The most reused of our datasets.
- **[EPU Quebec](https://sentometrics-research.com/download/epu-quebec/)** — a century of economic policy uncertainty from
  French-Canadian sources.
- **[EPU Belgium](https://sentometrics-research.com/download/epu-belgium/)** — daily, French- and Dutch-language, from 2001.
- **[US Topical Economic Sentiment](https://sentometrics-research.com/download/us-econ/)** — daily sentiment across economic topics.

## Topics that are open

These are directions we think are worth a thesis or a master's project. They are not assignments —
bring your own if you have one.

- **Sentiment under large language models.** What survives when the lexicon is replaced by a
  generative model? The aggregation and identification problems do not disappear; they change shape.
- **Concern indices beyond climate.** The MCCC recipe — risk plus sentiment, weighted by attention —
  has been extended to social risk. Other domains are open.
- **Text selection and coverage.** Which sources enter a corpus, and how coverage shifts over time,
  can move an index more than the sentiment method does.
- **Indices in languages other than English.** Our EPU work in French and Dutch showed the
  standard recipes do not transfer unchanged.
- **Monetising sentiment.** From index to implementable strategy, net of costs.
